I Will Never Climb Whitney Again (That's a Lie)

Reflection

I was never much of an all nighter kid, even when I had loads of work to do. I would diligently shut down my laptop at 1am and hop into bed. Sleep is probably one of my favorite things ever. So when I learned that I would be climbing Mt. Whitney, that too in one day, boy was I in for a treat. See, Whitney is technically only a 21-mile (round trip) hike, but when you take into account the 6,500 feet elevation gain, the glitter and sparkles tend to disappear.


The day I climbed Mt. Whitney, August 19, 2024, was probably the ONLY day I spent awake for 24 hours straight (technically closer to 26 hours). And I was one of the luckier ones. A few people in our group were up for nearly 30 hours, and hiking at that. Already, I was feeling the effects, finishing Whitney at the 20 hour mark (about 24 hours after I'd woken up), and as I say with every climb, I joked that I would NEVER climb it again. But, I would do it in a heartbeat. Truth be told, I don't remember the exhaustion, the irritation, or the misery from that hike. I swear the brain does something funny with your memories, especially when you're putting your body through hell like that.


Admittedly, I used my mom's AllTrails map for this hike, and what a relief that was. Most of the Whitney trail is well marked, but there is a particular region of the ascent (and descent) where the trail took us over unmarked boulders, and navigating that in the moonlight, while exhausted, was quite a challenge. I took charge of navigation, and was able to effectively pull us, as well as a few stragglers who were lost, all the way to Trail Camp. I felt like a little scout being sent out by George Washington to check the trail before reporting back to my army, as silly as that sounds.


And Trail Camp, what a unique place. A lot of backpackers climb Whitney over the course of two days and actually spend their night at Trail Camp. And how they do it, I have no idea. Trail Camp was SO windy, it blew my hash brown RIGHT out of my hands. Who knows where that hash brown is now. Trail Camp is also one of the last few places to get water before the infamous 99 switchbacks of Whitney.


The switchbacks were definitely quite difficult, especially for my mom, who has dealt with bad knees for a while now. This portion of the hike consisted of well-timed breaks and ample motivation for her (but also secretly for my dad and I too). The best part of the switchback portion was actually the stunning sunrise we witnessed. Within an hour we saw the face of granite change so many shades. The sun looked incredible rising out of the far far horizon. Probably somewhere in Nevada or Arizona.


Though, what I probably remember the most from that hike is the euphoria of reaching the Whitney Summit. The two mile stretch before the summit was absolute hell and it truly felt like there was no end to wild wind and large granite boulders. It was 10am when my family and I finally completed the ascent. An ascent that took us 10 hours and 30 minutes. And what an accomplishment it was, especially for my parents, both of whom have had health issues in their pasts. So climbing Whitney at that speed and their age. Incredible. And our descent was quite beautiful as well. While climbing up, we hiked under the moonlight, so we didn't get a chance to see the incredible Lone Pine Canyon's granite boulders, lush greenery, and roaring (more like whispering) river.
